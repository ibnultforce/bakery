Baking Basics

How to Raise a Sourdough Starter in 7 Days

By Elena Marsh · October 12, 2026 · 8 min read

Every loaf of sourdough we bake starts with Margot — a bubbling jar of flour and water that has lived in our bakehouse for twelve years. People often ask whether they can buy a piece of her. (You can! Ask at the counter.) But there's real joy in growing your own, and it's simpler than you think.

Here is the exact schedule we teach in our weekend bread classes.

What you'll need

  • A clean 1-litre glass jar with a loose lid
  • Whole-wheat or whole-rye flour (to start) and unbleached white bread flour (for feeding)
  • Filtered or room-temperature tap water left out overnight
  • A kitchen scale — volume measurements make this much harder
  • A rubber band to mark the starter's level
Baker's tip: Whole-grain flour carries more of the wild yeasts and bacteria you're trying to cultivate. Using it for the first two days gives your starter a head start.

The 7-day schedule

  1. Day 1 — Mix. Stir 50g whole-wheat flour with 50g water (about 26°C) into a thick paste. Cover loosely and leave somewhere warm.
  2. Day 2 — Wait. You may see a few bubbles, or nothing at all. Both are normal. Give it a stir and leave it alone.
  3. Day 3 — First feed. Discard all but 50g. Add 50g flour (half whole-wheat, half white) and 50g water. Mark the level with your rubber band.
  4. Day 4 — The false rise. Your starter may double dramatically, then go quiet. That's the wrong bacteria burning out — keep feeding once a day.
  5. Day 5 — Twice a day. Switch to two feeds, roughly 12 hours apart, with white flour only. Look for a sweeter, yoghurt-like smell.
  6. Day 6 — Rhythm. It should now rise predictably within 4–8 hours of feeding and smell pleasantly sour.
  7. Day 7 — Test it. If it reliably doubles within 6 hours and a spoonful floats in water, it's ready to bake with.
“A starter isn't an ingredient so much as a pet. Feed it, keep it warm, and it will reward you for years.”

Troubleshooting

Grey liquid on top? That's “hooch”, a sign your starter is hungry. Pour it off and feed more often.

Nothing happening? Temperature is usually the culprit. Try the top of your fridge or inside an oven with just the light on.

Pink or orange streaks? Unfortunately that's contamination. Start again with a freshly cleaned jar.

Keeping it alive

Once established, you can store your starter in the fridge and feed it once a week. Take it out the night before you bake, give it a feed, and it'll be lively by morning.
