Behind the Counter

The Secret Life of a Croissant: 3 Days, 27 Layers

By Tomás Rivera · September 28, 2026 · 6 min read

At 7am on a Saturday, our pastry case holds around 400 croissants. Most of them will be gone by ten. What our customers don't see is that each one of those crescents started its journey three days earlier.

Here's what happens in between.

Day 1 — The détrempe

It starts with the détrempe: a simple dough of flour, milk, sugar, salt, yeast and a little butter. We mix it just enough to bring it together — overworking it now makes for tough pastry later — then wrap it and let it rest in the fridge overnight.

Meanwhile, we prepare the butter block. We use a cultured butter with 82% butterfat, beaten and rolled into a perfect 19cm square between sheets of parchment. It needs to be cold but pliable, like modelling clay.

Why cultured butter? It's slightly tangy, which balances the sweetness of the dough, and its lower water content means crisper, more distinct layers.

Day 2 — Lamination

This is where the magic happens. The butter block is enclosed in the dough, then rolled and folded three times:

  1. The lock-in: butter is sealed inside the dough like an envelope.
  2. First fold (letter fold): rolled long, folded in thirds — 3 layers of butter.
  3. Second fold: rest 30 minutes, roll, fold again — 9 layers.
  4. Third fold: one final rest and fold — 27 layers.

Temperature is everything. If the butter gets too warm it melts into the dough; too cold and it shatters into shards. Our lamination room sits at a steady 16°C and we check the dough with a probe thermometer between every fold.

“You can't rush lamination. The dough tells you when it's ready — you just have to learn to listen.”

Day 3 — Shape, proof, bake

At 3am the dough is rolled to exactly 4mm thick and cut into long triangles. Each one is gently stretched, notched and rolled into its crescent by hand — a skilled baker can shape about 300 an hour.

The croissants then proof for around two and a half hours at 26°C, until they wobble like jelly when the tray is nudged. An egg wash, and into the oven at 190°C for 18 minutes.

The water in the butter turns to steam, pushing the layers apart and creating that airy honeycomb. The sugars in the dough caramelise into a deep amber crust. And by 7am, they're in the case — still crackling as they cool.

How to enjoy one properly

  • Eat it within 6 hours of baking for peak flakiness.
  • Day-old? Five minutes in a 180°C oven brings it back to life.
  • Never microwave a croissant. Please.
